Raising the village…

    Cheam Village

    Walk the old village in first person. Streets and buildings come live from OpenStreetMap; the ground, roof heights and trees come from the Environment Agency's 1 m LiDAR survey of England.

    Nothing is downloaded until you press the button.

    Each landmark is placed from its OpenStreetMap outline when one exists. Choose one to walk straight there; walk within a few metres to read its card.

    Build the village to list the shops, cafés and pubs mapped in OpenStreetMap.

    With an OS key, each building's recorded construction period, wall material and roof material set its look. The key is stored encrypted with the rest of your data.

    Quality changes apply after a quick reload (downloads are cached, so it's fast).

    What this page keeps

    Everything stays in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere except the map and LiDAR requests listed below, and there are no cookies, accounts or analytics.

    • Your settings, last position, the landmarks you've visited and any building edits you've made.
    • A cache of the OpenStreetMap and LiDAR downloads, so the village rebuilds without refetching.
    • All of it is encrypted with AES-GCM (256-bit) before it touches IndexedDB. The key is generated by the browser as non-extractable, so scripts can use it but can't read it out.

    Encryption protects the stored data at rest; anyone with full control of this browser profile can still open this page and use it.

    Checking storage…

    Network requests

    • cdn.jsdelivr.net: three.js, proj4, delaunator and geotiff, pinned to exact versions.
    • Overpass API (overpass-api.de, with two public mirrors as fallback): OpenStreetMap features.
    • environment.data.gov.uk: LiDAR DTM and DSM tiles (WCS).
    • s3.amazonaws.com (Terrarium elevation tiles): used only if the LiDAR service can't be reached.
    • services-eu1.arcgis.com: Historic England's National Heritage List (listed buildings).
    • api.os.uk: Ordnance Survey building details, only if you add your own key.

    How it's made

    The ground is the LiDAR digital terrain model. Each building's eaves and ridge are read from the LiDAR surface model inside its OpenStreetMap footprint, and the map is nudged a few metres to line up with the LiDAR before anything is built. Trees are found as peaks in the canopy height model (surface minus terrain) outside buildings. Walls, roofs and pavements are drawn by shaders: stretcher-bond brick, weatherboarding, coursed stone, half-timbering with leaded lights, clay tiles and slate.
